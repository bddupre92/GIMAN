## Key gaps identified

A concise synthesis of recurrent shortcomings visible across the included studies, emphasizing validation and mechanistic coverage. Each gap statement reflects patterns observed in the table above.

- External validation shortfalls  
  - **4 of 10 studies** are internal-only (Tier 0) and lack external cohort testing despite longitudinal aims. This includes small-sample DL temporal studies and DBS-response work where only internal resampling (LOO or repeated CV) was used [4] [8] [9] [10].  

- Mechanistic coverage  
  - Only **1 of 10 studies** used an explicitly mechanistic/dynamical modeling approach capable of simulating biological effects (Bayesian graphical/“mechanistic” model) [2]; the remaining 9 are purely data-driven ML approaches.  

- Small sample sizes and overfitting risk  
  - Several prognostic DL/time-series studies report **very small samples** (e.g., n=42; n=50) and rely on extensive internal resampling, raising overfitting/generalizability concerns [9] [10].  

- Heterogeneous comparators and reporting gaps  
  - Although most studies compare multiple ML algorithms or baseline clinical rules, the **validation-method wording and horizon details are inconsistently reported** in abstracts (some abstracts omit an explicit validation-protocol sentence), complicating assessment of reproducibility and tier assignment for some studies [4] [10].  

- Limited mechanistic interpretability for clinical translation  
  - Even studies with strong predictive performance rarely incorporate biologically grounded mechanistic models or deploy digital-twin style simulations, which limits ability to test intervention counterfactuals or to mechanistically interpret predictors beyond feature-importance statements [1] [3] [5] [7] [8] [9] [10].
