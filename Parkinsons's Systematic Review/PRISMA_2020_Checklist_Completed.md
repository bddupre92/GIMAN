| Synthesis methods (statistical synthesis) | 13d | Describe and justify synthesis methods | Section 2.7 provides detailed justification: **Meta-analysis not feasible**: Quantitative meta-analysis was precluded by (1) **outcome metric heterogeneity** (studies reported iAUC, AUC, sMAPE, F-measure, accuracy with no common metric across studies), and (2) **missing variance estimates** (0 of 6 comparative studies reported 95% confidence intervals or standard errors for intervention models; only point estimates were available). **Synthesis method used**: **Narrative synthesis** following Synthesis Without Meta-analysis (SWiM) guidelines, supplemented by **vote-counting** based on direction of effect. **Vote-counting method**: For each study, we classified the result as "Favors Dynamic" (dynamic model outperforms static baseline), "Favors Static" (static baseline outperforms dynamic model), or "No Difference" (performance within ±2% or statistically non-significant). We then tallied the number of studies in each category. **Effect size reporting**: For studies with quantitative comparisons, we computed absolute difference (Δmetric) and relative improvement (%). **Rationale**: This approach maximizes use of available data while acknowledging limitations. Vote-counting is explicitly discouraged by Cochrane when effect sizes are available, but we report both vote counts AND effect sizes to provide context. **Software**: No statistical software was used for synthesis (narrative synthesis performed manually). | Section 2.7 (Data Synthesis and Analysis), Section 3.4 (Synthesis of Results) | **Yes** |
| Synthesis methods (heterogeneity exploration) | 13e | Specify methods used to explore heterogeneity | Section 2.7 and Section 4.3 describe heterogeneity exploration: **Methods**: Because meta-analysis was not feasible, formal statistical tests for heterogeneity (I², τ²) were not applicable. | Synthesis methods (sensitivity analyses) | 13f | Report sensitivity analyses | Section 2.7 and Section 4.4 describe sensitivity analyses: **Sensitivity analysis 1**: Restricting to studies with external validation (Tier 2 only, n=4 of 6 comparative studies). **Result**: Effect sizes were more modest (median +6.4% relative improvement) compared to all studies (median +11.7%), suggesting validation tier moderates effect size. **Sensitivity analysis 2**: Restricting to studies with sample size ≥500 (n=3 of 6). **Result**: Effect sizes remained positive (+4.3% to +15%) but with wider range, suggesting sample size alone does not explain heterogeneity. **Sensitivity analysis 3**: Excluding studies at high risk of bias (n=2 excluded, leaving 4 studies). **Result**: All 4 remaining studies still favored dynamic models, suggesting risk of bias does not fully explain the observed pattern. **Pre-specification**: Sensitivity analyses 1 and 2 were pre-specified; sensitivity analysis 3 was post-hoc (identified as such in Section 4.4). **Interpretation**: Results were robust to validation tier and sample size restrictions, but effect sizes were attenuated in higher-quality studies, consistent with optimism bias in lower-tier validation. | Section 2.7 (Data Synthesis and Analysis), Section 4.4 (Sensitivity Analyses) | **Partial** (2 pre-specified, 1 post-hoc) |
| Reporting bias assessment | 14 | Specify methods used to assess risk of bias due to missing results | Section 2.8 describes reporting bias assessment: **Methods**: (1) **Funnel plot**: Not generated because fewer than 10 studies with quantitative comparisons (n=6) makes funnel plot interpretation unreliable. | Certainty assessment | 15 | Specify the tool used to assess certainty in the body of evidence | Section 2.9 describes certainty assessment: **Tool**: GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation) framework, adapted for prognostic model studies following GRADE guidance for prognostic studies. **Factors assessed**: (1) **Risk of bias** (PROBAST assessments), (2) **Inconsistency** (heterogeneity in effect direction and magnitude), (3) **Indirectness** (whether studies directly address the review question), (4) **Imprecision** (sample size, confidence interval width), (5) **Publication bias** (selective reporting, small-study effects). **Certainty levels**: High (further research very unlikely to change confidence in estimate), Moderate (further research likely to have important impact), Low (further research very likely to have important impact), Very Low (very uncertain about estimate). **Decision rules**: Started at "High" for observational prognostic studies, then downgraded one level for each serious concern or two levels for very serious concerns. **Adaptations**: Standard GRADE framework adapted by not penalizing observational design (appropriate for prognostic questions). **Reviewers**: Two independent reviewers assessed certainty; disagreements resolved by consensus. **Presentation**: GRADE Summary of Findings table (Table 4) presents certainty assessments for each outcome domain with explanations for downgrades. **Phrases**: Used standard GRADE phrases (e.g., "low certainty evidence suggests..."). | Section 2.9 (Certainty of Evidence Assessment), Section 3.7 (Certainty of Evidence), Table 4 | **Yes** |
