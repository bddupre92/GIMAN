### Example (Figure 10 - Forest Plot):
"Forest plot showing effect sizes with 95% confidence intervals for six comparative studies evaluating dynamic or complex models versus baseline approaches for Parkinson's disease prognosis. Effect size represents relative improvement in prediction performance (%), calculated as [(Intervention - Comparator) / Comparator] × 100. Positive values indicate intervention superiority; negative values indicate comparator superiority. Studies are color-coded by comparison type: dark green for dynamic versus static comparisons (n=2), steel blue for static versus static comparisons (n=4). Point estimates are shown as circles with size proportional to estimated study weight. Five of six studies (83%) favored the intervention model, with effect sizes ranging from -2.3% (slight disadvantage) to +28.9% (substantial advantage). The largest effects were observed for motor progression forecasting (+28.9%, Chaithanya et al. 2025, Tier 0 validation) and fall prediction (+18.8%, van Wegen et al. 2016, Tier 2 validation). One study showed minimal disadvantage for the intervention (-2.3%, Gao et al. 2018, comparing two static architectures). Confidence intervals are marked with asterisks (*) when estimated rather than reported (5/6 studies). Shaded regions indicate clinical significance thresholds: light green (5-10% improvement), dark green (>10% improvement). The heterogeneity barriers box notes that formal meta-analysis was precluded by different outcome metrics (iAUC, AUC, sMAPE, F-measure, Accuracy), missing variance estimates (0/6 studies reported 95% CI for intervention models), different prediction goals (progression, falls, cognitive decline), and different prediction horizons (6-36 months). While the visual pattern suggests consistent benefit for dynamic/complex models, the fragile evidence base (only 2 studies directly testing the hypothesis) and absence of reported confidence intervals prevent definitive conclusions. †Did not meet strict inclusion criteria but included for completeness."
